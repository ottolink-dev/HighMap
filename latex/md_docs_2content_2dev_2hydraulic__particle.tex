 This document details the simulation model, kinematics, sediment transport equations, and implementation of particle-\/based hydraulic erosion ({\ttfamily hydraulic\+\_\+particle}) in High\+Map.\hypertarget{md_docs_2content_2dev_2hydraulic__particle_autotoc_md35}{}\doxysection{\texorpdfstring{1. Overview}{1. Overview}}\label{md_docs_2content_2dev_2hydraulic__particle_autotoc_md35}
Hydraulic erosion models the carving of terrain by surface water runoff. In High\+Map, particle-\/based hydraulic erosion uses a Lagrangian formulation where virtual water droplets (particles) are spawned across the heightfield, navigate downslope according to local terrain kinematics, transport suspended sediment, and iteratively carve channels and deposit alluvial fans.

\hypertarget{md_docs_2content_2dev_2hydraulic__particle_autotoc_md36}{}\doxysection{\texorpdfstring{2. Particle Kinematics and Trajectories}{2. Particle Kinematics and Trajectories}}\label{md_docs_2content_2dev_2hydraulic__particle_autotoc_md36}
\hypertarget{md_docs_2content_2dev_2hydraulic__particle_autotoc_md37}{}\doxysubsection{\texorpdfstring{Surface Interpolation and Gradient Evaluation}{Surface Interpolation and Gradient Evaluation}}\label{md_docs_2content_2dev_2hydraulic__particle_autotoc_md37}
Each particle is defined by a continuous 2D position \$\textbackslash{}mathbf\{x\} = (x, y) \textbackslash{}in \mbox{[}0, N\+\_\+x -\/ 1\mbox{]} \textbackslash{}times \mbox{[}0, N\+\_\+y -\/ 1\mbox{]}\$. The integer grid coordinates \$(i, j)\$ and fractional offsets \$(u, v)\$ are given by\+:

\$\$i = \textbackslash{}lfloor x \textbackslash{}rfloor, \textbackslash{}quad j = \textbackslash{}lfloor y \textbackslash{}rfloor, \textbackslash{}quad u = x -\/ i, \textbackslash{}quad v = y -\/ j\$\$

Elevation \$z(\textbackslash{}mathbf\{x\})\$ is obtained through bilinear interpolation over the four enclosing heightfield vertices\+:

\$\$z(u, v) = (1 -\/ u)(1 -\/ v) z\+\_\+\{i, j\} + u (1 -\/ v) z\+\_\+\{i+1, j\} + (1 -\/ u) v z\+\_\+\{i, j+1\} + u v z\+\_\+\{i+1, j+1\}\$\$

The continuous terrain surface gradient \$\textbackslash{}nabla z = (g\+\_\+x, g\+\_\+y)\$ is computed analytically across the cell\+:

\$\$g\+\_\+x = (1 -\/ v)(z\+\_\+\{i+1, j\} -\/ z\+\_\+\{i, j\}) + v(z\+\_\+\{i+1, j+1\} -\/ z\+\_\+\{i, j+1\})\$\$

\$\$g\+\_\+y = (1 -\/ u)(z\+\_\+\{i, j+1\} -\/ z\+\_\+\{i, j\}) + u(z\+\_\+\{i+1, j+1\} -\/ z\+\_\+\{i+1, j\})\$\$\hypertarget{md_docs_2content_2dev_2hydraulic__particle_autotoc_md38}{}\doxysubsection{\texorpdfstring{Trajectory Integration and Momentum Blending}{Trajectory Integration and Momentum Blending}}\label{md_docs_2content_2dev_2hydraulic__particle_autotoc_md38}
The steepest downhill unit vector is \$-\/\textbackslash{}frac\{\textbackslash{}nabla z\}\{$|$\textbackslash{}nabla z$|$\}\$. To simulate fluid momentum and prevent abrupt artificial turning, the particle\textquotesingle{}s heading direction \$\textbackslash{}mathbf\{d\}\+\_\+t\$ blends the downhill gradient vector with its previous momentum direction \$\textbackslash{}mathbf\{d\}\+\_\+\{t-\/1\}\$ via inertia coefficient \$c\+\_\+\{\textbackslash{}text\{inertia\}\} \textbackslash{}in \mbox{[}0, 1\mbox{]}\$\+:

\$\$\textbackslash{}mathbf\{d\}\+\_\+t = \textbackslash{}mathrm\{normalize\}\textbackslash{}left((1 -\/ c\+\_\+\{\textbackslash{}text\{inertia\}\}) \textbackslash{}left(-\/\textbackslash{}frac\{\textbackslash{}nabla z\}\{$|$\textbackslash{}nabla z$|$\}\textbackslash{}right) + c\+\_\+\{\textbackslash{}text\{inertia\}\} \textbackslash{}, \textbackslash{}mathbf\{d\}\+\_\+\{t-\/1\}\textbackslash{}right)\$\$

Across flat plateaus or local minima where \$$|$\textbackslash{}nabla z$|$ \textbackslash{}le \textbackslash{}epsilon\+\_\+\{\textbackslash{}text\{slope\}\}\$, momentum dominates (\$c\+\_\+\{\textbackslash{}text\{eff\}\} = \textbackslash{}max(c\+\_\+\{\textbackslash{}text\{inertia\}\}, 0.\+9)\$), enabling particles to glide over shallow sills rather than stalling immediately.

Position integration advances the particle along its current heading\+:

\$\$\textbackslash{}mathbf\{x\}\+\_\+\{t+1\} = \textbackslash{}mathbf\{x\}\+\_\+t + \textbackslash{}\+Delta t \textbackslash{}, \textbackslash{}mathbf\{d\}\+\_\+t\$\$\hypertarget{md_docs_2content_2dev_2hydraulic__particle_autotoc_md39}{}\doxysubsection{\texorpdfstring{Velocity and Energy Conservation}{Velocity and Energy Conservation}}\label{md_docs_2content_2dev_2hydraulic__particle_autotoc_md39}
As a droplet moves from elevation \$z\+\_\+\{\textbackslash{}text\{prev\}\}\$ to \$z\+\_\+\{\textbackslash{}text\{curr\}\}\$, the elevation difference \$\textbackslash{}\+Delta z = z\+\_\+\{\textbackslash{}text\{prev\}\} -\/ z\+\_\+\{\textbackslash{}text\{curr\}\}\$ drives acceleration or deceleration under gravity coefficient \$g\$\+:

\$\$v\+\_\+\{t+1\} = \textbackslash{}sqrt\{\textbackslash{}max\textbackslash{}left(0, v\+\_\+t\texorpdfstring{$^\wedge$}{\string^}2 -\/ \textbackslash{}\+Delta z \textbackslash{}cdot g\+\_\+\{\textbackslash{}text\{eff\}\}\textbackslash{}right)\}\$\$

For downhill movement (\$\textbackslash{}\+Delta z \texorpdfstring{$>$}{>} 0\$), kinetic energy increases. For uphill climbs (\$\textbackslash{}\+Delta z \texorpdfstring{$<$}{<} 0\$, e.\+g., inertia-\/driven movement across a local dip), gravitational deceleration applies. Kinetic friction dampens velocity each step\+:

\$\$v\+\_\+\{t+1\} \textbackslash{}leftarrow v\+\_\+\{t+1\} \textbackslash{}cdot (1 -\/ \textbackslash{}mu\+\_\+\{\textbackslash{}text\{drag\}\})\$\$

Volume decreases due to evaporation\+:

\$\$\+V\+\_\+\{t+1\} = V\+\_\+t \textbackslash{}cdot (1 -\/ \textbackslash{}mu\+\_\+\{\textbackslash{}text\{evap\}\})\$\$\hypertarget{md_docs_2content_2dev_2hydraulic__particle_autotoc_md40}{}\doxysection{\texorpdfstring{3. Sediment Transport Dynamics}{3. Sediment Transport Dynamics}}\label{md_docs_2content_2dev_2hydraulic__particle_autotoc_md40}
At each step, the droplet carries an internal sediment load \$s\$. The maximum sediment capacity \$s\+\_\+c\$ is governed by the stream power equation\+:

\$\$s\+\_\+c = \textbackslash{}max\textbackslash{}left(0, c\+\_\+\{\textbackslash{}text\{capacity\}\} \textbackslash{}cdot V \textbackslash{}cdot v \textbackslash{}cdot \textbackslash{}\+Delta z\textbackslash{}right)\$\$

The transport imbalance \$\textbackslash{}\+Delta s\+\_\+c = \textbackslash{}\+Delta t (s\+\_\+c -\/ s)\$ determines whether erosion or deposition occurs.

\hypertarget{md_docs_2content_2dev_2hydraulic__particle_autotoc_md41}{}\doxysubsection{\texorpdfstring{Deposition}{Deposition}}\label{md_docs_2content_2dev_2hydraulic__particle_autotoc_md41}
When the particle slows down, reaches flatter slopes, or moves uphill (\$\textbackslash{}\+Delta s\+\_\+c \texorpdfstring{$<$}{<} 0\$ or \$\textbackslash{}\+Delta z \texorpdfstring{$<$}{<} 0\$), it drops sediment\+:

\$\$\textbackslash{}\+Delta s\+\_\+\{\textbackslash{}text\{drop\}\} = c\+\_\+\{\textbackslash{}text\{deposition\}\} \textbackslash{}min(s, \textbackslash{}text\{to\+\_\+deposit\})\$\$

\$\$s \textbackslash{}leftarrow s -\/ \textbackslash{}\+Delta s\+\_\+\{\textbackslash{}text\{drop\}\}\$\$

The dropped sediment is distributed across the grid cell using bilinear weights\+:

\$\$w\+\_\+\{00\} = (1 -\/ u)(1 -\/ v), \textbackslash{}quad w\+\_\+\{10\} = u(1 -\/ v), \textbackslash{}quad w\+\_\+\{01\} = (1 -\/ u)v, \textbackslash{}quad w\+\_\+\{11\} = uv\$\$

\$\$z\+\_\+\{i + \textbackslash{}delta\+\_\+i, j + \textbackslash{}delta\+\_\+j\} \textbackslash{}leftarrow z\+\_\+\{i + \textbackslash{}delta\+\_\+i, j + \textbackslash{}delta\+\_\+j\} + w\+\_\+\{\textbackslash{}delta\+\_\+i, \textbackslash{}delta\+\_\+j\} \textbackslash{}\+Delta s\+\_\+\{\textbackslash{}text\{drop\}\}\$\$\hypertarget{md_docs_2content_2dev_2hydraulic__particle_autotoc_md42}{}\doxysubsection{\texorpdfstring{Erosion}{Erosion}}\label{md_docs_2content_2dev_2hydraulic__particle_autotoc_md42}
When the flow is under-\/saturated (\$\textbackslash{}\+Delta s\+\_\+c \texorpdfstring{$>$}{>} 0\$ and \$\textbackslash{}\+Delta z \texorpdfstring{$>$}{>} 0\$), material is carved from the surface\+:

\$\$\textbackslash{}\+Delta s\+\_\+\{\textbackslash{}text\{lift\}\} = c\+\_\+\{\textbackslash{}text\{erosion\}\} \textbackslash{}min(\textbackslash{}\+Delta s\+\_\+c, \textbackslash{}\+Delta z)\$\$

\$\$s \textbackslash{}leftarrow s + \textbackslash{}\+Delta s\+\_\+\{\textbackslash{}text\{lift\}\}\$\$

Material is subtracted bilinearly from the surrounding vertices. When an optional bedrock map \$z\+\_\+\{\textbackslash{}text\{bedrock\}\}\$ is provided\+:
\begin{DoxyItemize}
\item Unconsolidated sediment (where \$z \texorpdfstring{$>$}{>} z\+\_\+\{\textbackslash{}text\{bedrock\}\}\$) is eroded up to 4 times faster than bedrock.
\item Subtraction is clamped to prevent digging below \$z\+\_\+\{\textbackslash{}text\{bedrock\}\}\$\+:
\end{DoxyItemize}

\$\$z\+\_\+\{i, j\} \textbackslash{}leftarrow \textbackslash{}max(z\+\_\+\{\textbackslash{}text\{bedrock\}, i, j\}, z\+\_\+\{i, j\})\$\$\hypertarget{md_docs_2content_2dev_2hydraulic__particle_autotoc_md43}{}\doxysection{\texorpdfstring{4.
